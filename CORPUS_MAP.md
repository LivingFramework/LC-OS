# LC-OS Corpus Map

**Purpose:** Provide a one-page map of how the LC-OS research corpus develops across papers, ledger, and supporting architecture.

LC-OS is best read as a cumulative research programme. Each paper extends, reframes, or formalises the previous layer.

---

## Research Sequence

```text
Core problem
Quiet failure in long-horizon human-AI collaboration:
context drift, memory decay, numerical divergence, trust fracture, boundary blur
        |
        v
Paper 1 - Foundations
Context engineering, canonical artefacts, A-controls, execution gates
        |
        v
Paper 2 - LC-OS Method
Lean Collaboration Operating System, Running Documents, Step Mode,
Challenge Protocol, Stability Ping, File Governance
        |
        v
Paper 3 - Failure and Repair
F1-F6 failure taxonomy, SDRN repair protocol, TraceSpec,
failure as a design object
        |
        v
Mahdi Ledger - Historical Trace
AI-authored account of the collaboration from inside the governed system
        |
        v
Paper 4 - Relational Layer
Trust, rupture, recommitment, dyadic ethics,
stability as capacity for visible repair
        |
        v
Paper 5 - Linguistic Governance
Language as micro-governance interface, scope drift signals,
repair language, behavioural anchors
        |
        v
Paper 6 - Governance Architecture
Full layered architecture, minimal stability conditions,
reliability as emergent system property
        |
        v
Paper 7 - Governed Distributed Cognition
Human + AI + artefacts as a distributed cognitive system,
governed cognitive loop, recoverability
        |
        v
Paper 8 - Validation Layer
Validation-centric architecture, structured adversarial evaluation,
validation as missing architectural layer
```

## One-Sentence Corpus Summary

LC-OS argues that long-horizon human-AI collaboration becomes reliable when context, failure, language, cognition, and validation are treated as governed architectural layers rather than left to model capability or conversational memory alone.
